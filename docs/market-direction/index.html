Research question

Can public news sentiment and macroeconomic conditions improve next-session SPY direction prediction beyond market features? This is a test of incremental information, followed by a separate assessment of whether a long/cash implementation survives assumed costs.

TARGET1[ close(t+1) / open(t+1) − 1 > 0 ]

The signal uses information through session t’s close. It is executed at the next open and closed at that session’s close. Zero-return sessions count as “not up.”

What the sentiment variable means

The San Francisco Fed’s Daily News Sentiment Index summarizes sentiment in economic news. It is a broad economic-news proxy—not a SPY-specific earnings signal, social-media feed or custom sentiment classifier.

The project engineers levels, rolling averages, changes and standardized values from that published index. It does not claim to have trained a language model or collected proprietary news.

Evaluation rules

  • Fixed original hyperparameters, with market-only, macro, sentiment and combined feature ablations.
  • Extension search and fresh-test dates committed before its evaluation.
  • Balanced accuracy and AUC measure discrimination; Brier score and log loss assess probability quality.
  • Net return, zero-rate Sharpe, drawdown, exposure and hit rate measure implementation.
  • Costs charged on every entry and exit; no free turnover for consecutive signals.
  • Block bootstrap intervals describe uncertainty; they are not multiple-testing-adjusted proof of alpha.

Limits & next research steps

  • One ETF and a short fresh sample cannot establish robustness across asset classes or firms.
  • Replace revised data with licensed point-in-time data before investment use.
  • Validate longer forecast horizons suited to slow-moving macro signals.
  • Collect forward paper-trading observations with immutable timestamps.
  • Estimate realized spreads, slippage, capacity and cash financing.
  • Independent model review should precede any deployment.
